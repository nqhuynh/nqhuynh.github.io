Alphabet

Vietnamese writes words with a Roman-based alphabet. There are 29 letters: 12 vowel letters and 17 consonant letters. The order will look familiar if you know the English alphabet.

A aĂ ăÂ âB bC c
D dĐ đE eÊ êG g
H hI iK kL lM m
N nO oÔ ôƠ ơP p
Q qR rS sT tU u
Ư ưV vX xY y

Vowel letters are bold: a, ă, â, e, ê, i, o, ô, ơ, u, ư, y.

Compare with English

  • Seven letters to notice: đ, ă, â, ê, ô, ơ, ư. The first is a consonant; the other six are vowel letters.
  • The standard 29-letter alphabet does not include f, j, w, z. You may still encounter these in foreign names or borrowed writing.

Vowels · Nguyên âm

Vietnamese has twelve vowel letters. We learn them in five groups, comparing the sound and the shape of the mouth within each group.

GroupVowel letters
1a · ă · â
2e · ê
3o · ô · ơ
4u · ư
5i · y

Three marks that belong to vowel letters

MarkWhere you see itWhat to notice
Curved markăDifferent from a.
Circumflex (“hat”)â, ê, ôPart of each vowel letter.
Hornơ, ưPart of the letter, not a tone mark.

A tone mark does a different job: a → â changes the vowel letter; a → à adds huyền. A word can have a marked vowel letter and a tone mark together, as in ố.

How to learn the sounds

Listen first. Watch the lips, jaw opening, and tongue position. Find the letter in the written word. Try the whole word, then compare it with the teacher’s model.

English spelling often gives one vowel letter several sounds. Vietnamese vowel patterns help us decode words, but combinations, endings, and regional pronunciation still matter. Use English sound clues as approximations.

Vowels: a - ă - â

Begin with a, ă, and â. These are three vowel letters, not three tone marks. Listen inside whole words and practice a small group at a time.

Pronouncing a

  • Start from an open, unrounded mouth. Let the tongue rest low.
  • The vowel in English father can be a rough clue. Keep the Vietnamese sound free of an English r ending.

Practice: ba · ta · ma. Listen first, then repeat.

Pronouncing ă

  • Hear a brief vowel inside ăn. Practice the whole syllable instead of stretching the vowel alone.
  • Compare its duration with a. Keep your voice comfortable; raising the pitch is not the way to explain this difference.

Practice: ăn · năm. Find ă in each word.

Pronouncing â

  • Use a short central vowel with a relaxed mouth, less open than a.
  • A brief English uh-like sound can help you start. Check it against the teacher’s sân; the English comparison is approximate.

Practice: sân · tâm. Listen to the vowel inside each word.

Vowels: e - ê

Pronouncing e

  • Keep the lips unrounded. Open the mouth more than for ê, with the tongue toward the front.
  • Think of the vowel in English bed as an approximate starting point. Listen to the Vietnamese example before copying it.

Practice: bé · mẹ. Watch the mouth opening.

Pronouncing ê

  • Keep the lips unrounded and the mouth less open than for e. The tongue sits higher toward the front.
  • Try the first vowel part of English day, then stop before its y-like glide. Hold the Vietnamese vowel steady.

Practice: bế · tên. Repeat the whole word.

Hear the difference

bé → bế

The initial and sắc tone stay the same; the vowel changes. Listen twice, then point to e or ê. Say the pair with your teacher. bé can mean small; bế means to carry a child in the arms.

English analogies vary across accents. Treat them as hints, not exact sound matches. Keep returning to a natural Vietnamese word.

Vowels: o - ô - ơ

Pronouncing o

  • Round the lips and keep the mouth relatively open.
  • The vowel in English law may help as an approximate sound clue. Check it against the teacher’s Vietnamese example.

Practice: to · con

Pronouncing ô

  • Keep the lips rounded with a smaller opening than o.
  • Think of the beginning vowel in English hope, without adding its closing glide.

Practice: tô · cô

Pronouncing ơ

  • Relax the lips into an unrounded position and use a central vowel.
  • Do not add an English r sound. Listen to the complete syllable.

Practice: tơ · bơ

Comparing o - ô - ơ

to · tô · tơ

Keep the initial and unmarked tone the same. Listen to the change in the vowel. Watch the rounded lips for o / ô and unrounded lips for ơ.

Vowels: u - ư

Pronouncing u

  • Round the lips closely and keep the vowel steady.
  • The vowel in English food can provide a rough starting point.

Practice: thu · tu

Pronouncing ư

  • Use an unrounded mouth position. Start with u, then relax the lip rounding while following the teacher’s model.
  • Lip movement is a starting cue; the tongue position must also adjust. Do not add an English r ending.

Practice: thư · tư

Comparing u - ư

thu · thư
tu · tư

Vowels: i - y

Pronouncing i and y

  • Keep the lips unrounded, with the tongue high toward the front.
  • The English vowel in see is an approximate clue.
  • These letters can represent a similar sound, but their spellings are not freely interchangeable.

Practice: bi · đi · ly

Compare the spelling

Find i in bi and y in ly. Listen to the whole word. Keep the written vowel letter when copying it.

Vowel combinations

A vowel combination belongs inside one syllable. Listen to the whole word before looking at its spelling. Some combinations move toward a closing sound; others have a changing vowel center.

PatternExamplesPractice idea
ao / eosao (star) · heo (pig)Say smoothly; tap once per syllable.
ai / aytai (ear) · tay (hand)The written y matters here. Compare the whole rhyme.
au / âusau (after) · sâu (worm)Hear the short vowel portion before the closing sound.
oi / ôi / ơivoi (elephant) · tôi (I) · bơi (swim)Hear the starting vowel, then glide.
ui / ưivui (happy) · gửi (send)Keep the spelling and copy the teacher’s whole-word model.
êu / iu / ưukêu (call) · chịu (endure) · cừu (sheep)Listen to the center and closing sound of each rhyme.
oa / oehoa (flower) · xòe (spread out)Hear the opening glide into the main vowel.
uê / uyHuế (city) · tuy (although)Follow the whole-word model; keep the written pattern.

Hear → find → blend

s + ao → sao
sờ - ao - sao

Point to the initial s and the rhyme ao. Blend, then return to the whole word. Try bao next. Add heo / leo only after ao is comfortable.

Listening and meaning

Draw a star and a pig. Hear sao or heo and point to the drawing. Then find the matching written word. Swap roles; avoid learning only the card positions.

Reading a combination with a tone mark

chào contains ao with huyền over a: ào. Keep the tone mark when writing. Read the word as one syllable.

The examples show spelling patterns; they are not minimal pairs. Their tones and endings can also differ. Do not read the final a in ia / ua / ưa as a separate full a syllable.

These spellings contain several vowel letters but still belong to a single syllable in the examples. Model the word as a whole; do not count vowel letters as syllables.

ui and uy are different

Compare tui (a familiar Southern form of I) with tuy (although). Hear the teacher’s examples; changing i to y changes the vowel pattern. These are listening examples, not phrases to memorize.

Practice

Choose two pictures: a kite, banana, or mango. Say the word, point to its written combination, and write it once with a model. Cover the model and try again if ready.

Southern speakers may realize some rhymes differently from Northern examples. Keep conventional spelling and follow the classroom teacher’s natural model.

Practice problems

Alphabet and vowel marks

1. List the twelve vowel letters.
2. Underline the vowel letters in ba, ăn, sân, mẹ, tên.
3. Explain the difference between a / â and a / à.

Vowel sounds

4. Identify the vowels in to, tô, tơ, thu, thư, bi, ly.
5. Listen to two words chosen by your teacher. Point to the vowel you hear, then repeat the whole word.
6. Compare bé and bế. Which vowel changes?

Combinations

7. Sort sao, heo, bao, leo under ao or eo.
8. Compare tai and tay. Circle their vowel patterns.
9. Find the vowel pattern in mưa, tiếng, chuối.
10. Write three practiced words from memory. Check every mark.

Homework

Practice three to five assigned words in three five-minute sessions: listen and repeat; read and identify vowel patterns; write and check. Use your notebook. If no Vietnamese-speaking helper is available, complete listening with a teacher next class.

Answers

1: a, ă, â, e, ê, i, o, ô, ơ, u, ư, y. 2: a, ă, â, e, ê. 3: â changes the vowel letter; à adds huyền. 4: o, ô, ơ, u, ư, i, y. 6: e changes to ê. 7: ao: sao/bao; eo: heo/leo. 8: ai / ay. 9: ưa, iê, uôi. Listening and original writing are checked with the teacher.
