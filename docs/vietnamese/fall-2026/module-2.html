Single consonants

The alphabet has 17 consonant letters: b, c, d, đ, g, h, k, l, m, n, p, q, r, s, t, v, x. Hear each inside a whole word before connecting the sound to its spelling.

LetterExampleStarting cue
bba · bênClose both lips, then open into the vowel.
ccá · conUse the back of the tongue. Keep the initial short.
ggà · gaUse the back of the tongue; follow the teacher’s whole-word model.
hho · hoaLet air flow into the vowel.
kkem · kêThe same basic initial sound as c, with a different spelling.
llá · lêTouch the tongue near the upper teeth.
mmẹ · muaClose the lips and let the sound resonate through the nose.
nno · nămThe tongue makes contact; the sound resonates through the nose.
ppinA less common initial, often in borrowed words. It also appears as an ending.
qqua · quêUsually written with u as qu. Learn the whole initial pattern.
tta · tôUse a short initial, without the strong puff of th.

Practice

ba · cá · gà · ho · kem · lá · mẹ · no · pin · qua · ta

Underline the first consonant spelling. Listen, then say the whole word. Letter names and sound-blending labels serve different purposes.

Southern consonant examples

LetterExampleListen for
dda · dêA common Southern model uses a y-like initial, unlike đ.
đđa · điA distinct tongue-contact initial. Compare da / đa.
rra · rauFollow the teacher’s natural r. Its realization varies among speakers.
ssân · senSome speakers keep a distinction from x; others merge them.
vvề · vuiMay be v-like or y-like in Southern speech. Keep v in writing.
xxe · xaHear the teacher’s s / x examples rather than assuming one pronunciation for all speakers.

Comparing d and đ

da · đa
dê · đê

da means skin; đa can name a banyan tree. dê means goat; đê means dike. The written initial changes while the unmarked tone stays the same.

Sound and spelling

A familiar home pronunciation may differ from the classroom example. Listen to the difference and keep conventional spelling. A spoken merger does not make the letters interchangeable.

Practice

da · đa · ra · sân · về · xe

Point to the word the teacher says, then take a speaking turn. Start with two choices if six choices are difficult.

Combined consonant spellings

Two or three letters can represent one initial consonant. Do not pronounce each letter separately.

Spelling comparisons

Find the complete initial before blending. The h in th, ph, gh, or ngh belongs to the spelling group.

Six written tone categories

Tone belongs to the syllable and can change a word’s meaning. Listen to each whole word before using a pitch description.

ToneExampleMeaningBroad listening cue
ngangmaghostUnmarked; relatively level.
sắcmámom / cheekRising.
huyềnmàbutLower and falling.
hỏimảgraveFollow the teacher’s dipping or curved contour.
ngãmãcodeOften shares the hỏi realization in Southern speech.
nặngmạrice seedlingLow and compact in the classroom model.

The examples keep m and a while changing the tone. These cues describe broad patterns; actual contours depend on the speaker and syllable.

Southern hỏi and ngã

Many Southern varieties merge hỏi and ngã in speech. Both written categories remain. mả and mã keep their different marks, even when pronounced alike. Northern examples commonly distinguish them.

Listen and repeat

ma · má · mà · mả · mã · mạ

Copy a natural, comfortable word. Pitch is only part of tone; duration and voice quality can also differ. Do not force the throat to produce a mark.

Tone marks and vowel marks

Vowel marks identify letters. Tone marks identify written tone categories. A syllable can contain both.

SpellingWhat to notice
a → âThe vowel letter changes.
a → àThe vowel remains a; huyền is added.
o → ô → ốThe circumflex belongs to ô. The acute mark adds sắc.
mẹThe dot below e is nặng.
mượnThe horns belong to the vowel letters; the dot marks nặng.

Comparing familiar words

ba · bà
ma · má
ban · bạn

ba can mean dad or three; bà means grandmother. ban can mean a committee; bạn means friend or you in suitable contexts. Listen to the tone as part of the whole syllable.

Written tone placement

Write the tone mark on the vowel shown by the word model. In chào, huyền appears over a. In tiếng, sắc appears over ê. In chuối, sắc appears over ô. These examples do not mean that the first written vowel always receives the mark.

Practice

bà · bạn · ghế · tiếng · chuối

Circle the tone mark and underline the vowel letters. Copy a word, cover the model, and write it again.

Endings and đánh vần

Tiếng Việt

Một tiếng (âm tiết) có vần và thanh điệu; có thể có âm đầu. Vần có âm chính và có thể có âm đệm, âm cuối. Ví dụ: tiếng bạn có âm đầu b, vần an và thanh nặng. Vần an gồm âm chính a và âm cuối n. Tiếng ăn không có phụ âm đầu; vần ăn gồm âm chính ă và âm cuối n, mang thanh ngang.

English

A syllable has a rhyme and a tone; it may also have an initial consonant. The rhyme contains the main vowel and may include a medial glide and a final sound. For example, bạn has the initial b, the rhyme an, and the nặng tone. The rhyme an contains the main vowel a and the final consonant n. The syllable ăn has no initial consonant; its rhyme ăn contains the main vowel ă and the final consonant n, with the ngang tone. Here, “rhyme” means the vowel part of a syllable together with any medial glide and ending.

Build and blend

bờ - a - ba
ba - huyền - bà

Hear the word, find its parts, blend them, and say the whole word again. Sound-blending labels are teaching tools, not English letter names.

Common consonant endings

Ending lettersExamplesNotice
m, n, ng, nhcam · con · ong · anhNasal endings; hear the whole rhyme.
p, t, c, chhộp · mát · học · sáchStop endings; do not add an extra uh after the word.

Ending pronunciations can vary across Southern and Northern varieties. Keep conventional spelling. Standard stop-final syllables with these consonant endings use sắc or nặng.

Read the patterns

Mẹ mua chuối.

Find m in mẹ, ua in mua, ch and uôi in chuối, then read the sentence: Mom buys bananas.

Practice problems

Consonants and spellings

1. Underline each initial: ba, đa, thư, nghe, phở, già.
2. Which spellings share a basic initial sound: c/k, g/gh, ng/ngh?
3. Choose the spelling: ___ế (g/gh); ___e (ng/ngh).
4. Listen to da or đa, then ta or tha. Point to the word and repeat it.

Tones and syllables

5. Name the tones in bà, má, mẹ, mả, mã.
6. Split chào and bạn into initial, rhyme, and tone.
7. Find the consonant ending in cam, con, học, sách.
8. Identify the vowel mark and tone mark in ố.
9. Read Mẹ mua chuối. Find one combined initial and one vowel combination.
10. Study ba, bà, bạn, thư, ghế. Cover the model and write three words.

Listening practice

Listen twice to one word from each set, point to the spelling, then echo the whole word. A partner can choose the next word.

da / đa
ta / tha
ba / bà
ma / má

Homework

Practice three to five assigned words in three five-minute sessions: listen and repeat; read and identify initials, rhymes, and tones; write and check. Use a notebook. If no Vietnamese-speaking helper is available, complete listening with a teacher next class.

Answers and references

Answers

1. b, đ, th, ngh, ph, gi.
2. All three pairs.
3. ghế; nghe.
5. huyền, sắc, nặng, hỏi, ngã.
6. ch + ao + huyền; b + an + nặng.
7. m, n, c, ch.
8. Circumflex identifies ô; acute mark identifies sắc.
9. ch; ua or uôi.
